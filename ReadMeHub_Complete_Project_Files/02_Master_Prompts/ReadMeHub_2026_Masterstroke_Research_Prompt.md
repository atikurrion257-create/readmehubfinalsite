# READMEHUB 2026 — ZERO-BASED MASTERSTROKE RESEARCH PROMPT

## Purpose

Conduct a complete, evidence-based investigation for building ReadMeHub from zero into a sustainable digital publisher, recommendation, discovery, decision-support, affiliate, advertising, and commerce-oriented platform.

Do not treat ReadMeHub as merely an SEO blog.

Do not assume the proposed business model or 13-category structure is correct. Determine what the 2026 market actually supports.

## Critical Zero-Based Rule

START FROM THE 2026 MARKET.

Do not use previous ReadMeHub strategy, competitor lists, keyword lists, category priorities, monetization assumptions, or strategic conclusions as evidence.

If old ReadMeHub materials are supplied later, they may only be used AFTER fresh research for a separate comparison/audit. They must not anchor the initial research.

If evidence contradicts the owner's idea, say so clearly.

If evidence is insufficient, say INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE.

Never manufacture certainty.

## Mission

Determine the most realistic path:

ZERO → MARKET VALIDATION → POSITIONING → ARCHITECTURE → WEBSITE BUILD → CONTENT → INDEXATION → FIRST VISITORS → RETURNING USERS → NEWSLETTER → AFFILIATE/CPA CONVERSIONS → ADVERTISING → DIRECT PARTNERSHIPS → REPEATABLE REVENUE → SCALING → AI-ASSISTED OPERATIONS.

Do not guarantee rankings, traffic, AdSense approval, affiliate approval, revenue, advertiser partnerships, or AI citations.

## 2. Competitor & Publisher Intelligence

Build a discovery pool of approximately 100–150+ relevant publisher/site/business candidates where feasible.

Classify direct competitors, broad publishers, affiliate publishers, review publishers, comparison publishers, deals/coupon publishers, consumer recommendation publishers, technology publishers, software/SaaS publishers, vertical publishers, SEO-first publishers, small publishers, mid-size publishers, large publishers, and discovery/recommendation platforms.

Select approximately 30–40 strategically relevant entities for deep analysis, including approximately 5–10 closest structural/direct competitors and 10–15 strategic benchmarks. Do not force numbers if a category genuinely has fewer relevant entities.

Discover competitors using Google Search/SERPs, AI search, category-specific searches, publisher discovery, reputable industry sources, SEO/traffic intelligence where accessible, affiliate/publisher ecosystems, and small/mid-size publisher discovery.

Do not make individual vendor blogs the primary competitor set.

For each deep competitor analyze audience, geography, categories, content model, content types, SEO/search strategy, keyword strategy, SERP strategy, AI-search visibility, original research, testing/review methodology, editorial standards, authorship, freshness/update frequency, information architecture, UX, navigation, internal linking, technical SEO, performance, accessibility, newsletter, distribution, affiliate model, advertising, CPA/lead generation, direct partnerships, sponsorship, deals, commercial pages, trust signals, disclosures, strengths, weaknesses, content gaps, keyword gaps, SERP gaps, UX gaps, monetization gaps, AI-search gaps, and operational weaknesses.

Study small → growing → established publisher paths where evidence exists.

## 3. Search, SERP & Keyword Intelligence

Research search intent, SERP composition, organic results, AI Overviews, AI Mode, featured snippets, People Also Ask, shopping results, ads, Reddit/forums, YouTube/video results, comparison sites, publishers, vendors, local results where relevant, SERP volatility, commercial intent, freshness requirements, ranking difficulty, and authority requirements.

Identify head terms, mid-tail, long-tail, problem queries, use-case queries, comparison, alternatives, best, pricing, deal, coupon, review, decision, and how-to queries.

Build keyword → intent → page-type mapping.

Every meaningful intent should have one primary URL owner unless a documented reason exists for multiple pages.

Identify cannibalization, overlap, thin variants, consolidation opportunities, and pages that should not exist.

## 4. AI Search & Zero-Click Survival

Research Google AI Overviews, Google AI Mode, AI answer engines, citation behavior, source selection, entity recognition, zero-click behavior, AI referrals, and click-through opportunities.

Determine which content AI can easily answer, which content still creates reasons to click, what can become AI-answer-resistant, what original information could earn citation value, and what interactive functionality can create clicks.

Evaluate original research, unique datasets, comparisons, testing evidence, screenshots, decision frameworks, calculators, tools, databases, price/deal verification, and structured entity data.

Do not claim secret AI SEO tricks.

## 5. Content Moat & First-Hand Evidence

Research hands-on testing, product/software testing, screenshots, workflows, setup guides, price verification, feature verification, experiments, benchmarks, original comparisons, original research, change logs, updates, and decision frameworks.

1. Commodity information
2. Synthesized information

Never claim testing, use, measurement, verification, purchase, contact, or experience without evidence.
